How much does mobile tyre fitting cost in the UK?

A straight answer on what goes into the price, how it compares to a garage visit, and what to check before you book.

If you've never used a mobile tyre fitting service before, the pricing can feel like a bit of a mystery — garages usually have a price list on the wall, but a callout to your driveway or the roadside doesn't work quite the same way. Here's what actually determines the cost, so you know what you're paying for.

What affects the price

Mobile tyre fitting prices in the UK are built from a few separate elements, and understanding each one helps you spot a fair quote from an inflated one:

  • The tyre itself. Budget, mid-range and premium tyre brands vary significantly in price, and this is usually the biggest single factor. A budget tyre on a small hatchback will cost far less than a premium tyre on a larger SUV.
  • Tyre size. Larger wheels and wider tyres generally cost more than standard sizes, partly because they're more expensive to stock and source.
  • The callout itself. This covers the fitter travelling to you with the equipment needed to do the job safely and properly on site — this is the main difference versus driving to a garage yourself.
  • Time and location. Late-night, weekend, or hard-to-reach locations can sometimes carry a premium, since it affects how the job is resourced.
  • Repair vs replacement. A straightforward puncture repair is typically cheaper than a full tyre replacement, when the damage allows for a repair at all.

Mobile fitting vs a garage: what's the actual trade-off?

A garage can sometimes offer slightly lower prices on the tyre itself, purely because they're not factoring in a callout. But that ignores the real cost of a flat tyre: getting the vehicle to the garage in the first place. If you can't safely drive on the tyre — which is often the case with a sidewall puncture or a full blowout — you're looking at a tow to the garage before they can even start, and that cost usually dwarfs any saving on the tyre price.

Mobile fitting is priced to cover the whole job in one call: no separate recovery fee, no time off work to sit in a garage waiting room, and no risk of driving on a tyre that isn't safe.

How to avoid overpaying

  • Get the price confirmed before booking, not after the fitter has already arrived. Any legitimate provider will do this over the phone or via a quote form.
  • Ask whether a repair is possible before assuming you need a full replacement — a competent fitter will always check this first, since it's usually the cheaper and more sustainable option when it's safe to do.
  • Compare like-for-like tyres. A quote for a budget tyre and a quote for a premium tyre will obviously differ — make sure you're comparing the same tyre brand and size.
  • Ask what's included. Disposal of your old tyre, a safety check, and a fair assessment of repair vs. replace should be standard, not an upsell.

When a repair is enough

Not every puncture needs a new tyre. As a general guide, punctures, nail holes or cuts up to 6mm on the tread are usually repairable, provided the tyre still has legal tread depth and the sidewall is undamaged. Anything larger, any sidewall damage, or tread worn below the legal wear indicators means the tyre needs replacing rather than repairing. A good mobile fitter will always check this on site and explain their reasoning — not default to the more expensive option.

The bottom line

Mobile tyre fitting isn't inherently more expensive than a garage — it's priced to cover a service a garage doesn't offer: coming to you, wherever you are, without you needing to drive on a damaged tyre. The best way to know exactly what you'll pay is to get a straight quote before booking, based on your specific vehicle and tyre.
